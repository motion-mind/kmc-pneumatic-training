Unit 05

Troubleshooting

Pneumatic troubleshooting is not guesswork; it is a walk down the signal chain with a gauge. Measure at the right points, in the right order, and the fault localizes itself. This unit gives you the method, the pressure interpretations, and decision trees for the symptoms you will actually see.

The method: isolate the signal chain

The loop is a chain, and every link can fail: main air, thermostat, controller, actuator, and airflow sensor. Work upstream to downstream and prove each link before moving on.

1. Main air at M
15–30 psi and steady? If not, stop. Nothing downstream can be trusted.
2. Thermostat output at T
Does it move 3–15 psi as you change the setpoint, and in the correct direction?
3. Branch output at B
Does the controller vary the branch to the actuator in response to T and the flow reading?
4. Actuator and damper
Does the damper stroke full open to full close without binding? Any diaphragm leak?
5. Velocity sensor and flow
Is the ΔP sensible, and does it change when airflow changes? Are H and L correct, and is the pickup clean?

Two rules make this fast:

  • Fix supply first. A steady 20 psi main air removes the most common cause of everything.
  • If many devices fail together, it is not the devices. Look at the compressor, dryer, filters, and main regulator.

Decision tree — the box delivers too little air (zone won't cool)

Is the thermostat actually calling for cooling?
If T is at minimum (or the room is satisfied), the box is behaving. Check the thermostat setpoint, calibration, and location before touching the box.
Is there adequate main air at M and a real signal at T?
No → repair the supply or the thermostat/reset signal.
Does the damper stroke fully open when commanded?
No → binding linkage, leaking actuator, or not enough pressure to stroke. Leak-test and free the linkage.
Is there enough upstream duct static from the AHU?
No → the box cannot deliver air the AHU is not supplying. Escalate to the air-handling system.
Is the velocity sensor giving a sensible ΔP that changes with flow?
No → clean the pickup (disconnect it first), verify H/L, check for kinked or leaking tubing.
Is HI STAT (max flow) set too low, or the reset type wrong so it is holding minimum?
Re-verify the reset type from KMC's table, then re-calibrate the maximum per Unit 04.

Decision tree — the box delivers too much air (zone over-cools or fights reheat)

Is the thermostat satisfied? Is T actually at minimum?
A satisfied space should drive the box toward minimum. If T is not dropping, the thermostat is the problem.
Is the reset type correct for the primary air and thermostat action?
Wrong reset type makes a satisfied box hold maximum instead of minimum. This is the classic reversed-control fault.
Is the damper action (N.O./N.C.) selected correctly?
Wrong selection drives the damper the wrong way for a given branch pressure.
Is LO STAT set correctly, and not past zero into negative reset?
Re-check the minimum calibration and the zero-minimum caution.
Is the airflow sensor reading low (telling the controller to open more)?
A blocked or mis-placed pickup under-reads flow, so the controller over-drives the damper. Verify against a Magnehelic and the box chart.

Decision tree — reversed control

Airflow increases on a heating call, or decreases on a cooling call.
Stop calibrating. Backwards control is almost always a configuration error, not a failed part.
Re-apply KMC's reset-type table.
Cooling + DA = direct; cooling + RA = reverse; heating + DA = reverse; heating + RA = direct.
Verify the thermostat's actual action at the sensor.
Do not trust the label — prove it with a gauge as the temperature changes.
Verify the damper action selection.
Confirm the N.O./N.C. arrows align exactly, then re-calibrate min and max.

Decision tree — hunting or oscillation

Is main air steady?
A surging or undersized supply makes every device hunt. Watch the gauge under load.
Is there moisture or oil in the air?
Contamination causes sticking and drift. Fix the supply — servicing one box will not help.
Any leak in the tubing or actuator diaphragm?
Leaks add a pressure-dependent error. Leak-test with a squeeze bulb.
Is the thermostat throttling range very narrow?
A narrow range reacts to noise and hunts. Match the device's intended range.
Is the damper or linkage loose or binding?
Mechanical slop in the linkage shows up as limit-cycle oscillation.
Is something else sequencing on the spring range instead of the reset range?
A P-E switch or reheat valve triggered at the wrong point can fight the airflow loop.

Decision tree — the damper will not move

Good main air at M?
No → supply problem. Yes → continue.
Does branch pressure at B change when the command changes?
No → controller or signal problem. Yes → the air is getting there, so look at the actuator.
Leaking actuator diaphragm, binding linkage, or insufficient force?
Leak-test the diaphragm and fittings; disconnect the linkage and stroke the damper by hand to feel for binding.

System-wide versus single-box

Scope the problem before you start. It changes where you begin.

Many boxes, same behavior

  • Building main air: compressor, dryer, filters, separators, main regulator.
  • An AHU problem: static pressure, fan, or a closed fire/smoke damper serving a whole branch.
  • A shared reset signal or time clock affecting a group.

One box only

  • That box's thermostat: calibration, location, blocked nozzle.
  • That box's controller: reset type, damper action, min/max, negative reset.
  • That box's actuator, linkage, tubing, and velocity sensor.

Phantom faults you can create yourself

Before you replace a part, check that you did not cause the symptom
  • Calibrating off the mounting plane. The CSC-3000 is position sensitive. Bench-calibrate then rotate, and min/max are wrong.
  • Plugging the port instead of the tubing. When you remove the T signal, plug the tubing — not the port.
  • Turning LO STAT fully clockwise. Creates negative reset and a narrowed span that looks like a bad controller.
  • Blowing out a connected sensor. Disconnect it from the controller first.
  • Using a generic airflow chart. KMC warns the illustration chart is not for calibration. Use the chart on the box.
  • Low-range Magnehelic on main air. Destroys the gauge and tells you nothing.
  • Chasing the thermostat when the location is wrong. No calibration fixes a thermostat in a draft or in direct sun.
