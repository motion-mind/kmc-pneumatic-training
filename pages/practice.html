Unit 06

Practice, calculators & scenarios

Reading about pneumatic control is not the same as doing it. Use the calculators to build intuition for flow and reset behavior, work the scenarios out loud before revealing the analysis, and finish with the mixed quiz.

Calculator — velocity pressure to airflow

The flow through a VAV inlet varies with the square root of the velocity pressure. Every box chart encodes the same relationship with a constant specific to that box's inlet size and sensor.

Where does K come from?

Only from the airflow chart affixed to the specific box. The default value here is illustrative. The point of the exercise is seeing that doubling ΔP does not double CFM — it increases flow by about 1.41×, because flow follows the square root.

Calculator — reset setpoint from the thermostat signal

This models how the CSC-3000 turns a thermostat branch pressure into a flow setpoint, using the reset start point, the reset span, and the LO/HI STAT settings.

Try these to feel how the controller behaves:

  • Set type to direct, start 8, span 5, min 300, max 1200. Move the thermostat output from 3 to 13 psi and watch the setpoint hold at minimum below 8, ramp between 8 and 13, and hold at maximum above 13.
  • Switch to reverse with the same numbers. Now the box holds maximum below 8 psi and resets down to minimum above 13 psi — the exact behavior that looks "backwards" if the reset type is wrong.
  • Change the span to 10 and notice the box responds more gradually. This is why KMC says to leave the 5 psi factory span alone.

Field scenarios

Cover the analysis, work each one down the signal chain, then reveal. There is often more than one right first move, but the order is what matters.

Scenario 1 — A zone that will not cool, box pinned at maximum

A south-facing office is 78°F with the thermostat set to 72°F. At the box, the damper is wide open and airflow will not increase further. The thermostat is a direct-acting cooling thermostat on cooling primary air.

Work it: What are your first three measurements, and what would each result tell you?

Analysis: The box is already at maximum, so the question is whether "maximum" is real or apparent. First, main air at M — confirm the controller has a valid supply. Second, the thermostat output at T — is it actually calling near 15 psi? Third, upstream duct static and the actual airflow against the box chart.

If T is high and airflow is genuinely at the maximum you calibrated, then the box is doing its job and the zone load exceeds design — the AHU static, reheat, or the load itself is the issue. If T is low while the space is hot, the thermostat is the problem (calibration, location, or a blocked nozzle). If T is high but the measured flow is below the chart's maximum, suspect a dirty or mis-placed velocity pickup, a partially stroking damper, or HI STAT set low.

Scenario 2 — A box that never modulates, thermostat has no effect

The box sits at full airflow all day. Changing the room thermostat from 65°F to 80°F changes nothing. Main air at M reads 20 psi.

Work it: How do you separate a thermostat problem from a controller problem?

Analysis: Put a gauge on T at the controller and work the thermostat through its range. If T does not move, the problem is the thermostat or its line — the controller is faithfully following a false signal. If T does move 3–15 psi and the branch at B still does not respond, the issue is in the controller or the airflow loop.

Then check the configuration: the wrong reset type and the wrong damper action both produce a box that ignores the thermostat in a way that looks like a dead controller. Finally, check for a negative-reset condition from a LO STAT knob cranked past zero, and confirm the velocity sensor is not badly under-reading so the controller thinks it must open further.

Scenario 3 — Reheat runs while the box is cooling

A zone with a hot-water reheat coil is uncomfortable: hot and cold air appear to be fighting. The reheat valve opens, then the airflow rises, and the space oscillates.

Work it: What sequencing mistake fits these symptoms exactly?

Analysis: KMC's guidance is specific: sequencing with other controllers, valves, or pneumatic-electric relays must use the controller's reset range, not the actuator's spring range. If the reheat valve or a P-E switch is triggered off the actuator spring range, it opens at a point that does not correspond to the flow setpoint, so reheat comes on while the box is still delivering maximum cooling air. Verify where the reheat signal actually originates, then resequence it to switch on the reset range at the intended reset pressure.

Scenario 4 — Every box on a floor is sluggish and drifting

Over a few weeks, boxes across a whole floor become slow to respond and their setpoints drift. A few thermostats seem to stick. Nothing was changed in the building automation system.

Work it: Why is servicing individual boxes the wrong first move?

Analysis: Simultaneous, gradual degradation across many independent devices points at the shared air supply. KMC warns that oil or moisture contamination will eventually cause device failure. Check the compressor, the dryer (is it actually drying?), the filters and separators, and the main regulator. If the air is wet or oily, no amount of nozzle cleaning will hold.

Fix the supply, then clean or replace affected devices. Sample the air at a low point in the system — that is where water collects.

Scenario 5 — A replacement controller was installed and the flows are wrong

An old CSC-2008 was replaced with a CSC-3021. The box now under-delivers at maximum and behaves differently than before. The installer says the new controller was "set the same as the old one."

Work it: What did the installer almost certainly miss?

Analysis: KMC's cross-reference is explicit that after replacing the controller, adjustments and calibration will be necessary. The min and max must be re-commissioned on the new device, in its final mounting plane, using the box's own airflow chart. The reset start and span must be verified against the intended sequence, and the damper action must match the requirement.

"Set the same as the old one" fails because the new controller's knobs, its factory reset start (3, 8, or 10 psi depending on model), and its precise calibration are not interchangeable with the old device's settings. Start from the design min/max and recalibrate.
