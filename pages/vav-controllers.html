Unit 03

VAV box controllers — KMC CSC-3000 series

The KMC CSC-3000 is a sub-master reset volume controller. It takes two inputs — a 3–15 psi reset signal from the room thermostat and a velocity pressure from the airflow sensor in the box inlet — and positions a damper to hold a flow setpoint. Separate knobs set the minimum and maximum airflow, and the thermostat signal resets the flow between them.

The control loop in one picture

A VAV box controller is really a flow-control loop. The thermostat does not command a damper position directly; it commands a flow setpoint. The controller continuously measures actual flow and drives the damper until measured flow matches the setpoint.

Room thermostat → port T
3–15 psi reset signal sets where the flow target sits between minimum and maximum
Velocity sensor in the inlet → ports H and L
differential pressure proportional to airflow
CSC-3000 compares the two
sets a flow target and modulates the branch output
Port B → damper actuator
damper moves until measured flow equals the target

Reset type: the decision that comes first

Before you touch a knob, decide whether the application needs direct reset or reverse reset. It depends on the temperature of the primary air entering the box and on whether the room thermostat is direct or reverse acting. KMC's table is the authority:

Primary airThermostatReset type
CoolingDirect acting (DA)Direct reset
CoolingReverse acting (RA)Reverse reset
HeatingDirect acting (DA)Reverse reset
HeatingReverse acting (RA)Direct reset

Get this wrong and the box controls backwards — more airflow on a heating call, less on a cooling call. It is one of the most common "failed controller" calls that turns out to be a configuration error.

What direct and reverse reset look like

  • Direct reset: below the reset start point the controller holds LO STAT = minimum flow. Above the start point, over the reset span, it resets from minimum up to HI STAT = maximum flow.
  • Reverse reset: below the reset start point the controller holds LO STAT = maximum flow. Above the start point, over the reset span, it resets from maximum down to HI STAT = minimum flow.

Reset start point

The reset start point is the thermostat pressure at which the controller begins to reset from LO STAT toward HI STAT.

  • Factory settings: models ship with a reset start of 3, 8, or 10 psi.
  • Field adjustable: 0–10 psi on all models.
  • Usually leave it alone. KMC notes the standard settings are correct for most applications.

Adjusting the reset start point

  1. Set the thermostat pressure to the desired start point (for example 3 psi) at port T.
  2. Remove the rubber plug at port G and read pressure there with a 0–30 psi gauge (needs 5/32" O.D. tubing).
  3. Turn the reset start control at the top of the controller with a small flat-blade screwdriver — counterclockwise to increase, clockwise to decrease — until the pressure at G is just beginning to move off zero.
  4. If it will not come in, revisit the reset span before condemning the controller.

Reset span

The reset span is the thermostat pressure change above the start point that moves the flow setpoint from one extreme to the other.

  • Factory setting: 5 psi.
  • Field adjustable: 0–10 psi.
  • KMC's advice is firm: leave it at 5 psi unless genuinely necessary. Changing it may force you to redo the min/max calibration.

Adjusting the reset span

  1. Drive the thermostat above its working range — 20 psi is best.
  2. Attach a pressure gauge to port G (5/32" O.D. tubing).
  3. Turn the reset span control at the bottom of the controller — counterclockwise to increase, clockwise to decrease — until the pressure at G equals the desired span.
Start point and span, together

Think of it as a window on the thermostat's 3–15 psi signal. The start point is where the window opens; the span is how wide it is. With a start of 8 and a span of 5, the box resets between 8 and 13 psi. The reset-setpoint calculator on the Practice page lets you play with these numbers.

Damper action

The CSC-3000's damper action is factory set at Normally Open (N.O.). To change to Normally Closed (N.C.):

  1. Loosen the damper selection screw.
  2. Turn the selection dial clockwise until the "NC" arrow aligns with the "DAMPER" arrow. Alignment accuracy matters — make it exact.
  3. Tighten the selection screw. About 2–4 in-lb; snug but not enough to strip the plastic.

The controller's damper action must agree with the physical damper so that increasing flow always means the same direction of travel.

Minimum and maximum airflow

Two knobs set the flow envelope: LO STAT ΔP (center dial) and HI STAT ΔP (right dial). They are set in terms of the sensor's velocity pressure, read on a Magnehelic gauge and converted to CFM with the box's chart. Which one is minimum and which is maximum depends on the reset type — see the table above.

Do not skip the reset-type decision

Roughly half of all "the min and max are backwards" calls are simply a direct/reset mismatch. Deciding the reset type first tells you unambiguously whether LO STAT is the minimum or the maximum. See Unit 04 for the full step-by-step procedure.

Cautions that prevent callbacks

  • Position sensitive. Mount and calibrate in the same horizontal or vertical plane. Calibrating on a bench and installing vertically throws off min and max.
  • Sequence on the reset range, not the spring range. KMC is explicit: any sequencing with other controllers, valves, or pneumatic-electric relays must use the controller's reset range, not the actuator's spring range.
  • Zero minimum. Do not turn the LO STAT knob fully clockwise. Past zero it goes into negative reset, which the controller must overcome before reset begins, narrowing the effective span. To get zero, adjust until the damper just cracks open, then back off about a quarter turn.
  • Patience. The output responds slowly. Wait 20–30 seconds after each adjustment for the flow to stabilize.
  • Control air only. Oil or moisture contamination leads to eventual failure.
  • Never blow out a connected sensor — disconnect it from the controller first.
